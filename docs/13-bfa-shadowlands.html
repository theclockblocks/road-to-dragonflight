XIIIBattle for Azeroth & Shadowlands

The War, the Whisper, and the Grave

Teldrassil burns, the last Old God rises, the sky is torn open — and Sylvanas's long story ends in the afterlife

The two most turbulent expansions in the game's history — the faction war's ugliest hour, the last Old God's bid for the world, and a detour into death itself that divided the playerbase and that Dragonflight deliberately walks away from. You need the events; you can skip the metaphysics.

Battle for Azeroth: the war for the world's blood

With Azerite promising weapons beyond imagining, Warchief Sylvanas strikes first: her armies burn the night elves' World Tree home of Teldrassil with thousands of civilians inside — the setting's darkest single act by a faction leader, and one the story never lets the Horde launder. The Alliance answers by besieging her capital, the ruins of Lordaeron, which she blights and abandons. Both sides recruit island nations — the Alliance the seafaring kingdom of Kul Tiras, the Horde the ancient troll empire of Zandalar — and the war grinds through naval campaigns, assassinations, and atrocities, until the Horde's own conscience revolts. The old soldier Varok Saurfang challenges Sylvanas to ritual single combat before both armies; she kills him, but when he goads her into it she says the truth out loud — "the Horde is nothing" to her, all of them merely fuel — and her own faction abandons her on the spot. She flies away with her real plan intact. The factions, exhausted, sign an armistice.

Beneath the war runs the whisper. Queen Azshara — ten thousand years a servant of N'Zoth — drags both navies to the sea floor and, through her own gloriously spiteful defeat in her Eternal Palace, achieves exactly what she wanted: the shattering of the last Old God's prison. N'Zoth — a drowned god, a vast squid-like bulk of eyes, maws, and cabled tendrils, each eye opening slowly as if amused — rises, seats his Black Empire's capital of Ny'alotha over Pandaria's scarred vale, and begins rewriting reality toward the Void's old dream of a corrupted world-soul. Armored in a titan-forged countermeasure, the heroes carry the fight into his waking nightmare-city, and the last of the four Old Gods is burned out of the universe. The Void Lords' ten-thousand-year plan on Azeroth dies with him — on the surface, at least.

The sky torn open

Then Sylvanas plays her hand. She rides to Icecrown, defeats the jailer Lich King Bolvar, and — rather than take the crown — shatters the Helm of Domination, tearing open the sky above Northrend into the realm of Death itself. The armistice-weary world watches its heroes, Anduin among them, be dragged bodily into the Shadowlands.

Shadowlands: the machine of death

The afterlife is revealed as a cosmic machinery of soul-triage — kyrian ferrymen, ancient forest rebirth-gods, honor-bound war-realms, vampiric sin-eaters — all of it starving, because every soul is being funneled instead into the torture-pit of the Maw. Its warden: Zovaal, the Jailer — a bald grey giant with molten eyes, wearing his prison-chains as ornaments, a glowing void in his chest where a sigil was torn out; a deposed and banished arbiter of Death who claims, with contested credibility, that nearly every catastrophe in this chronicle (the Helm, Frostmourne, the Lich King, and more) was a link in his design. His goal: reach the hidden workshop of reality's makers and rewrite existence into obedience. He dominates Anduin's body and will — the setting's kindest king made into its most tragic weapon — and for a time wields him as a new Arthas.

Sylvanas's whole story comes due here. Her bargain with the Jailer, struck in the despair after her death and centuries of torment, collapses when he finally states his endgame — not freedom from a rigged system of fate, but universal domination. She turns on him; the severed noble half of her soul is restored; and after his destruction at the raid's end, she is sentenced not to oblivion but to labor: to free, one by one, every soul her long war sent into the Maw. Anduin, freed but hollowed, walks away from his crown to wander. The heroes return home through the mended sky.

Out of universe: Shadowlands' cosmic scope-creep and retcons were badly received, and Blizzard said so almost openly in what came next. The story deliberately comes home — to soil, to dragons, to characters. That homecoming is the final chapter.
