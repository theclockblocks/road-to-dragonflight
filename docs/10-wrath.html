XWrath of the Lich King

The War Against the Lich King

Northrend, the madness in Ulduar, the Aspect who broke — and the fall of Arthas

The silence at the top of the world ends. Arthas wakes, the Scourge attacks every capital at once, and both factions launch full invasions of Northrend. This expansion is the payoff of everything Warcraft III set up, and it's still widely called the peak of the game's early era.

Three wars in one

The war against the Scourge grinds through haunted fjords and plagued tundra toward Icecrown Citadel. Its darkest hour is the battle at Angrathar the Wrathgate, where a renegade Forsaken faction unleashes a new plague on Scourge and living alike — killing Horde and Alliance heroes en masse, nearly shattering the factions' cold peace into open war, and forcing Sylvanas to retake her own capital from her mutineers. Trust between (and inside) the factions never fully recovers.

The war under the earth is the horror movie. Beneath the titan machine-city of Ulduar, the keepers' ancient wards are found broken from within: the Old God Yogg-Saron — a god that is mostly mouths, a heaving mass of green-black flesh and grins in the dark — imprisoned there since Chapter II, has long since corrupted its own jailers. Adventurers fight through the mind-bent keepers and put the Old God down in its cell — the second of the four Old Gods to be destroyed, and a live demonstration that the titans' prisons are failing.

The Nexus War is the dragon tragedy. Malygos, Aspect of Magic — driven half-mad ten thousand years earlier when Deathwing annihilated his flight, only recently healed — surveys the reckless magic of mortals and declares a simple verdict: revoke it. His blue flight begins severing the world's arcane arteries and killing mortal spellcasters wholesale. The other dragonflights, led by Alexstrasza, side with the mortals against their own brother. Malygos dies in his own sanctum at mortal and dragon hands — the first Aspect to fall, by the queen's own anguished order. Remember her grief; Dragonflight is built on it.

The Frozen Throne

The final assault on Icecrown Citadel — a black metal spire rising from the glacier, visible for miles, warm nowhere — climbs through Arthas's trophy-hall of horrors — including the raised corpse of Sylvanas's own sister-in-arms, and Frostmourne's chamber, where the trapped soul of his father waits. At the summit, the Lich King springs his real plan: he has let the world's champions come to him, intending to raise the strongest of them as his generals. He kills the entire raid — and is undone by mercy planted decades earlier: the spirit of Uther had revealed that a fragment of the good prince might remain, and the paladin Tirion Fordring, frozen and praying, is answered. Frostmourne shatters; the imprisoned souls it drank turn on their captor; and Arthas Menethil dies at last, asking his father's ghost if it is finally over.

It isn't, quite. Without a Lich King, the mindless Scourge would scour the world unchecked — someone must wear the crown. The fallen Alliance hero Bolvar Fordragon, kept alive-in-torment by dragonfire, takes the Helm of Domination and the Frozen Throne as the new, jailer Lich King: not a conqueror but a warden, meant to be forgotten. File that arrangement away — its breaking is Chapter XIII.
