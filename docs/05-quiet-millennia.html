VThe ten thousand quiet years

The Quiet Millennia

The elves splinter, the mortal kingdoms rise, and one bloodline is cursed to guard the world

Ten thousand years pass between the Sundering and the first Warcraft game. They are quiet only by comparison. This chapter sets every playing piece on the board.

The elven diaspora

Not all night elves accepted the new ascetic order. Exiled for refusing to abandon arcane magic, a faction of Highborne sailed east to the Eastern Kingdoms and founded the kingdom of Quel'Thalas — a realm of eternal autumn, gold-leafed forests threaded with white-and-gold spires. There they became the high elves — smaller, sun-loving, day-walking — and, using a stolen vial of the old Well's waters, kindled a new fount of power at their kingdom's heart: the Sunwell. Its radiance fed every high elf's soul. That dependency is a loaded gun on the wall; it fires in Chapter VIII.

Trolls, humans, and the first human empire

The elves had built their kingdom on land the ancient troll empires considered theirs, and the trolls — the Eastern Kingdoms' oldest power, ancestors from whom the night elves themselves had long ago evolved — made war to reclaim it. Desperate, the high elves turned to a rising young people: humanity, descendants of the curse-of-flesh titan-forged, now united under the empire of Arathor. In exchange for aid, the elves taught a hundred humans magic. Together they broke the troll empires — and human civilization never looked back. Arathor eventually fractured into the Seven Kingdoms you'll hear named for the rest of the story: proud Lordaeron in the north, mage-ruled Dalaran, seafaring Kul Tiras, mountain Alterac, knightly Stromgarde, forested Gilneas, and far to the south, Stormwind.

The dwarves delved their mountain city of Ironforge, the gnomes built beside them, and out west on Kalimdor the nomadic tauren followed the herds while the night elves kept their forests. The pandaren, walled off behind impenetrable mists since before the Sundering, kept their own hidden peace with the Sha. The board is nearly set.

The Guardian of Tirisfal

Human mages learned quickly that demons still slipped into the world through the cracks. In secret, elven and human magi founded a council that would pour the power of many into a single champion: the Guardian of Tirisfal, one mortal empowered far beyond mortal limits to fight the Legion's infiltrators in the shadows. For generations the title passed down — until it reached Aegwynn, a Guardian so mighty she struck down an avatar of Sargeras himself. Or so she believed. In truth, the fallen titan's spirit slipped from the broken avatar into the one place she never looked: her own unborn son.

That son was Medivh — the last Guardian, the most powerful mortal alive, and, from before his birth, the sleeper vessel of Sargeras. He grew up alongside two friends in Stormwind: the future king Llane, and the future war-hero Anduin Lothar. The stage is set. All that's missing is an army — and Sargeras, wearing Medivh's face, knew exactly where to find one.
