IXWorld of Warcraft & The Burning Crusade

The World at War

Old Gods stirring, the reopened Dark Portal, and the fall of Illidan — vanilla WoW and TBC

World of Warcraft opens four years after Hyjal. The Alliance (now led from rebuilt Stormwind under King Varian Wrynn) and Thrall's Horde (from the new orc capital of Orgrimmar) keep a cold, grudging peace — and adventurers, meaning the players, become the era's real protagonists.

Vanilla: old evils stir

The original game's stories are mostly the buried past clawing at the floorboards. Deathwing's scheming children — Onyxia, secretly whispering in Stormwind's court in human guise, and Nefarian, breeding abomination-dragons in his volcanic fortress — are hunted down. The Elemental Lord Ragnaros, anciently banished beneath the mountains, is destroyed in his Molten Core. In the desert of Silithus, the war against the insectoid servants of the Old God C'Thun reignites, and the god itself — imprisoned since Chapter II, never fully healed — is put down inside its temple-prison of Ahn'Qiraj. And above the Plaguelands, Kel'Thuzad's floating necropolis Naxxramas is cast down. Every one of these is the deep past refusing to stay buried; none of them is the future. That arrives through a portal.

The Burning Crusade: through the Dark Portal

The Dark Portal reopens, and both factions march into Outland — the shattered husk of Draenor from Chapter VII — where Illidan rules from the Black Temple with his lieutenants: the naga sea-witch Lady Vashj — six arms, a mane of living serpents — and the blood elf prince Kael'thas, golden-haired and impossibly poised, three captive green suns orbiting his shoulders. Two great redemption-and-damnation arcs collide here:

  • The draenei return. Velen's people — the genocide's survivors, some now aboard a stolen dimension-ship that crashes on Azeroth — join the Alliance, bringing the story of Argus and the naaru with them.
  • Kael'thas falls. The blood elf prince, promised salvation for his people's magic-addiction, sells them to the Legion instead — even capturing a naaru, M'uru, for his people to feed on. The blood elves who stay loyal to their homeland join the Horde; their prince becomes their bitterest enemy.

The campaign rolls through Vashj's reservoir-fortress and Kael'thas's Tempest Keep, and finally through the Black Temple itself, where Illidan — betrayer, betrayed, never quite villain enough to hate — falls to the adventurers he sneered at. His last words, "the huntsmaster is worthless," are less famous than his first: "You are not prepared!", the expansion's tagline and still the most quoted line in the game.

The Sunwell reborn

The finale closes the wound of Chapter VIII. A broken Kael'thas drags the dying naaru M'uru to the ruins of the Sunwell and begins summoning Kil'jaeden bodily into the world through it. The summoning is broken, the Deceiver is hurled back — and in the aftermath, Velen pours the naaru's radiant heart into the defiled fount. The Sunwell reignites, no longer merely arcane but touched by the Light. The blood elves' long grief finally has a dawn — a redemption that becomes the seed of the much later Midnight expansion.
