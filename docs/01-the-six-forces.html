The Six Forces

The cosmology Warcraft never explains on screen — and why every villain makes sense once you know it

The Warcraft universe is a tug-of-war between six primal forces, arranged in opposing pairs: Order and Disorder, Light and Void, Life and Death. Each force has its native realm, its energies, and its cosmic faction. The games almost never lay this out directly — it comes mostly from a companion book series called World of Warcraft: Chronicle — but almost every villain you will meet makes sense once you can place them on this map.

Order — the titans

Titans are god-like beings of living metal and stone whose souls gestate inside planets. A planet with a titan soul growing within it is called a world-soul. This is the single most important fact in all of Warcraft: Azeroth, the planet the game is played on, is a world-soul — and by every account the most powerful one ever found. Nearly every major villain in the franchise is, one way or another, fighting over this sleeping unborn god.

The awakened titans formed a council of six called the Pantheon, led by the Highfather Aman'Thul. They roamed the cosmos searching for slumbering kin, imposing order on chaotic worlds as they went. Because their sheer size and power could crush a fragile world-soul by accident, they rarely acted directly; instead they forged armies of servants from stone and metal — the titan-forged — and elevated the greatest of these into keepers to act in their stead.

Disorder — Sargeras and the Burning Legion

Sargeras was the Pantheon's champion — its foremost warrior, tasked with hunting demons across creation. In the course of that duty he interrogated captured servants of the Void and learned of their plan (below): to corrupt a world-soul and birth a god of their own. He drew a conclusion that broke him. A Void-corrupted Dark Titan would be worse than no universe at all — therefore every world that might host one must burn first.

When the Pantheon recoiled from this logic, Sargeras shattered them, scattered their spirits, and raised the demons he had spent eons imprisoning into an endless army: the Burning Legion. Among his most valuable recruits were two brilliant leaders of the eredar people, Kil'jaeden the Deceiver and Archimonde the Defiler — names that will haunt every later chapter. For millennia the Legion scoured the cosmos of life, a guardian who had decided annihilation was mercy. Its ultimate target was always Azeroth.

Void — the Void Lords and the Old Gods

Outside reality dwell the Void Lords: entities of pure hungering shadow who cannot easily enter the physical universe. Their plan is patient and horrifying: corrupt a world-soul while it sleeps, so that when it wakes, it wakes as theirs — an infinitely powerful Dark Titan of the Void. Their weapons are the Old Gods: mountain-sized parasites of tentacle and maw, hurled blindly across the cosmos at promising worlds like biological warheads. Four of them struck Azeroth. Their tools are whispers, madness, and time — corruption measured in ten-thousand-year increments.

Light, Life, and Death

The remaining three forces matter later, so file them away now. The Light is the source of the Holy magic wielded by priests and paladins; its purest children are the benevolent wind-chime beings called the naaru. Life flows through nature, the Wild Gods, and the Emerald Dream. And Death has an entire bureaucratic afterlife-machine of its own, the Shadowlands — which the story mostly ignores until it becomes the entire plot of one very controversial expansion.

New to Warcraft? Don't memorize the chart. Just hold onto three things: the planet is a sleeping god; the Void wants to corrupt her; and the fallen titan Sargeras decided it was better to burn her than lose her. Everything else in this site is the fallout.
