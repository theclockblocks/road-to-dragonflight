VIIIYear 20 – 21 after the Portal

The Third War

The plague, the fall of Arthas, the rebirth of the Horde, and the last stand at Mount Hyjal

The events of Warcraft III — the single most important stretch of story in the franchise, and the foundation of everything World of Warcraft is. Take this chapter slowly.

The plague of undeath

The Lich King worked exactly as designed — and the design was logistics, not sorcery. Plague-tainted grain moved into the villages of Lordaeron through ordinary supply chains, sat in ordinary granaries, and was eaten by people who had no reason to be suspicious of their own bread. Running the operation was Kel'Thuzad, an archmage of the Kirin Tor who had gone looking for forbidden knowledge and found something looking back. Expelled for it, he went to the ice, knelt, and came home to found the Cult of the Damned — which recruited the living by promising them immortality, and then kept the promise. Investigating the outbreak: the kingdom's crown prince Arthas Menethil — a broad-shouldered golden-haired paladin in the silver-and-blue of his kingdom; the realm's golden son, mentored by the great paladin Uther the Lightbringer, in love with the young mage Jaina Proudmoore. What follows is the most famous fall in the setting, and it happens step by defensible step.

The fall of Arthas

The ramp matters more than the cliff. For months before anything famous happened, Arthas was competent, and thorough, and always one village too late — riding in to burn what was already dead, doing everything correctly and saving nobody. Something in him began to bend around that.

At the city of Stratholme it broke. The shipped grain had already been eaten; every citizen in the city was hours from rising as Scourge. Arthas ordered the city killed before it could turn. Uther refused — so Arthas relieved his own mentor of command and called him a coward, and Jaina turned away and left. And here is the part the setting never lets you off the hook for: he may well have been right. There was no version of that day in which those people lived. There was also no version in which a man kills a city one street at a time and walks out the far side still himself. Both are true at once, and the Culling of Stratholme is the place they meet.

From there the descent steepened. He pursued the plague's demon-herald Mal'Ganis to Northrend — and when his father recalled the expedition home, he burned his own fleet so that his men could not leave, then arranged for a mercenary captain to take the blame. On the ice, a spirit at his own soldiers' cairn warned him that the runeblade Frostmourne would cost him his soul. He read the warning. He understood it. He took the blade anyway — because by then, saving his people and winning had become the same word in his mouth.

He sailed home a monster — golden hair gone bone-white, skin corpse-pale, the silver plate of a paladin exchanged for black armor, eyes burning ice-blue — and the court cheered him the whole length of the throne room before he reached his father and killed him. He killed Uther afterward, over the urn holding that father's ashes, and raised Kel'Thuzad as an undead lich. Lordaeron ceased to exist as a nation, its lands rotting into the Plaguelands.

Then Arthas turned east and did the thing the elves have never forgiven. He carved through Quel'Thalas, murdered its ranger-general Sylvanas Windrunner — a golden-haired elf in forest leathers, her people's finest archer — and, refusing her the dignity of death, tore her spirit out and enslaved it as a banshee in his service. (When she later reclaims a body, it is changed: ashen skin, eyes glowing ember-red under a dark hood — beautiful the way a knife is. That is the Sylvanas every later chapter means.) Something close to ninety percent of that people died in the invasion. He defiled the Sunwell to resurrect Kel'Thuzad, poisoning the fount that fed every high elf's soul. The survivors, led by prince Kael'thas Sunstrider, renamed themselves the sin'dorei — blood elves — in mourning, and began looking for anything to feed the addiction the Sunwell's loss left behind.

The Scourge then annihilated the mage-city of Dalaran's defenses and used its books to summon the demon lord Archimonde into the world. He repaid the summoning by lifting the city's magic bodily out of it and leaving the ruin behind. The Legion's third great play for Azeroth had begun — with the Scourge as its plough.

Meanwhile: the Horde reborn

In the same years, the slave-gladiator Thrall — a green orc built like a fortress door, with rare storm-blue eyes, in scavenged black plate with the warhammer Doomhammer across his back — escaped his captivity. He had been raised by Aedelas Blackmoore, the camp commander — not out of mercy but as an investment. Blackmoore's plan was to educate an orc properly, train him as a champion, and one day put him at the head of a slave army. He got the education exactly right and every other part of it exactly wrong: Thrall used all of it to walk out the front gate.

He found the Frostwolf exiles in the northern mountains — his father's clan, driven there for refusing the demon blood two chapters ago — and learned from them the thing his people had traded away. Shamanism is not commanding the elements; it is asking them, and being answered because you asked. Then he broke open the internment camps and woke the orcs out of their grey despair, not with demon blood but with their own old faith. It was not a conquest. It was a recovery.

Guided by a mysterious prophet — Medivh's redeemed spirit, spending his second existence saving the people he had let through the portal in his first — Thrall led his new Horde across the sea to Kalimdor. There they found the tauren being run down by marauding centaur and stopped to help, which became the Horde's oldest and steadiest friendship. And there they collided with the night elves. Grommash Hellscream — wiry and ferocious, his jaw tattooed black, swinging an axe with holes bored through the blade so it screams as it cuts; first to drink the demon's blood a generation before — was cutting timber in Ashenvale under constant night elf ambush when he drank from a demon-tainted fountain and fell to the old curse a second time. He slew the demigod Cenarius. Thrall hunted him down and broke the possession by force, dragging him back to himself — which is what makes what follows a decision rather than a lucky accident. Grom went after Mannoroth and died killing him, breaking the blood-curse on his people forever. "The blood haze has lifted," Thrall tells him as he dies. "You are free." The Horde's debt was its own again.

Mount Hyjal

Archimonde marched on Mount Hyjal to devour the World Tree Nordrassil and the power of the Well beneath it. What met him was a coalition with no business existing, and it is worth being specific about what each part of it had to swallow. Jaina's humans had evacuated an entire continent on the word of a prophet none of them could identify, and had watched Arthas destroy their kingdom on the way out. Thrall's orcs had spent fifteen years in Alliance cages. Tyrande and Malfurion's night elves had lost their demigod to an orc's axe roughly a month earlier.

They fought in relay — humans, then orcs, then night elves — each force spent in its turn to buy the next one time. And Malfurion was not merely delaying: he was choosing where Archimonde would be standing. Through the whole battle the mountain's spirits gathered silently in the branches above, and when the demon lord finally climbed the tree to take what he had come for, they detonated.

Archimonde was destroyed. The night elves paid with the tree's blessing and, with it, their immortality — a people ten thousand years old who woke the next morning mortal and had to begin working out what that meant. The Legion was broken for a generation, and the fragile idea of Horde–Alliance coexistence was born on that mountainside.

Aftershocks

Three loose threads from these years drive the next decade. Illidan, freed from his ten-thousand-year prison, accepted a commission from Kil'jaeden to destroy the weakened Lich King, gathered the naga and Kael'thas's desperate blood elves, and fled to Outland when he failed — half villain, half jilted weapon. Sylvanas tore her will free as the Lich King's power flickered, seized the plague-lands of fallen Lordaeron, and forged the free-willed undead into a new people: the Forsaken, who joined the Horde out of shared outcast pragmatism. And Arthas, racing Illidan to the Frozen Throne, shattered the ice, donned the Helm of Domination, and fused with Ner'zhul's spirit: the prince and the Lich King became one being. Then he sat upon the Frozen Throne — and for years, the top of the world went silent.
