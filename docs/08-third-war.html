VIIIYear 20 – 21 after the Portal

The Third War

The plague, the fall of Arthas, the rebirth of the Horde, and the last stand at Mount Hyjal

The events of Warcraft III — the single most important stretch of story in the franchise, and the foundation of everything World of Warcraft is. Take this chapter slowly.

The plague of undeath

The Lich King worked exactly as designed. Through a death-cult led by the fallen archmage Kel'Thuzad, plague-tainted grain flowed into the villages of Lordaeron, and the dead began to rise. Investigating the outbreak: the kingdom's crown prince Arthas Menethil — a broad-shouldered golden-haired paladin in the silver-and-blue of his kingdom; the realm's golden son, mentored by the great paladin Uther the Lightbringer, in love with the young mage Jaina Proudmoore. What follows is the most famous fall in the setting, and it happens step by defensible step.

The fall of Arthas

At the city of Stratholme, Arthas discovered the shipped grain had already been eaten: every citizen was hours from rising as Scourge. His solution — over Uther's horrified refusal and as Jaina turned away — was the Culling of Stratholme: kill the city before it turned. From there the descent steepened. He pursued the plague's demon-herald to Northrend; on the ice, obsession swallowed him. Warned that the runeblade Frostmourne would cost him his soul, he took it anyway. The blade bound him to the Lich King's will, and the prince sailed home a monster — golden hair gone bone-white, skin corpse-pale, the silver plate of a paladin exchanged for black armor, eyes burning ice-blue: he murdered his father on the throne, killed Uther, and raised Kel'Thuzad as an undead lich. Lordaeron ceased to exist as a nation, its lands rotting into the Plaguelands.

Then Arthas turned east and did the thing the elves have never forgiven. He carved through Quel'Thalas, murdered its ranger-general Sylvanas Windrunner — a golden-haired elf in forest leathers, her people's finest archer — and, refusing her the dignity of death, tore her spirit out and enslaved it as a banshee in his service. (When she later reclaims a body, it is changed: ashen skin, eyes glowing ember-red under a dark hood — beautiful the way a knife is. That is the Sylvanas every later chapter means.) He defiled the Sunwell to resurrect Kel'Thuzad, poisoning the fount that fed every high elf's soul. The survivors, led by prince Kael'thas Sunstrider, renamed themselves the sin'dorei — blood elves — in mourning, and began looking for anything to feed the addiction the Sunwell's loss left behind.

The Scourge then annihilated the mage-city of Dalaran's defenses and used its books to summon the demon lord Archimonde into the world. The Legion's third great play for Azeroth had begun — with the Scourge as its plough.

Meanwhile: the Horde reborn

In the same years, the slave-gladiator Thrall — a green orc built like a fortress door, with rare storm-blue eyes, in scavenged black plate with the warhammer Doomhammer across his back — escaped, found the old Frostwolf exiles, learned the shamanism his people had abandoned, and did the impossible: he broke open the internment camps and woke the orcs from their gray despair — not with demon blood, but with their old faith. Guided by a mysterious prophet (Medivh's redeemed spirit, making amends for his possessed life's work), Thrall led his new Horde across the sea to Kalimdor. There they befriended the tauren and collided with the night elves. The war-chief Grommash Hellscream — wiry and ferocious, his jaw tattooed black, swinging an axe with holes bored through the blade so it screams as it cuts; first to drink the demon's blood a generation before — fell to it again, slew the demigod Cenarius, and then, in the franchise's great redemption scene, died killing Mannoroth himself, breaking the blood-curse on his people forever. "The blood haze has lifted," Thrall is told. The Horde's debt was its own again.

Mount Hyjal

Archimonde marched on Mount Hyjal to devour the World Tree Nordrassil and the power of the Well beneath it. At Medivh's urging, Jaina's human survivors, Thrall's Horde, and Tyrande and Malfurion's night elves — enemies days earlier — held the slopes together in the setting's defining last stand, buying time until Malfurion sprang his trap: the tree's own spirits detonated against the demon lord. Archimonde was destroyed; the night elves paid with the tree's blessing, and with it their immortality. The Legion was broken for a generation — and the fragile idea of Horde–Alliance coexistence was born on that mountainside.

Aftershocks

Three loose threads from these years drive the next decade. Illidan, freed from his ten-thousand-year prison, accepted a commission from Kil'jaeden to destroy the weakened Lich King, gathered the naga and Kael'thas's desperate blood elves, and fled to Outland when he failed — half villain, half jilted weapon. Sylvanas tore her will free as the Lich King's power flickered, seized the plague-lands of fallen Lordaeron, and forged the free-willed undead into a new people: the Forsaken, who joined the Horde out of shared outcast pragmatism. And Arthas, racing Illidan to the Frozen Throne, shattered the ice, donned the Helm of Domination, and fused with Ner'zhul's spirit: the prince and the Lich King became one being. Then he sat upon the Frozen Throne — and for years, the top of the world went silent.
