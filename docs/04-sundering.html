IVTen thousand years ago

Azshara and the Sundering

The golden empire of the night elves, the first Legion invasion, and the breaking of the world

The Well of Eternity — the warded wound from Chapter II — sat at the heart of the world's single ancient continent. The elves who settled its shores drank of its power and became the night elves, the kaldorei: violet-skinned and towering, with luminous silver or amber eyes, at home under moonlight in old-growth forest. Their civilization became the mightiest the mortal world has ever known. Then it burned in a single generation.

The Light of Lights

Queen Azshara — golden-eyed, silver-tressed, called the most beautiful being ever born; brilliant, vain, magnificent, genuinely adored — ruled the kaldorei at their golden height, surrounded by her arcane elite, the Highborne. Their ever-more-reckless drawing on the Well blazed like a beacon into the dark between the stars, and Sargeras saw it. Whispering through the Well, he seduced the queen and her court with promises of a perfected world. They opened a portal in the Well's depths, and for the first time the Burning Legion poured into Azeroth.

The War of the Ancients

The war that followed drew in everyone. Three young night elves stepped into legend: the druid Malfurion Stormrage, stag-antlered and green-bearded, the first mortal to learn Cenarius's arts; the priestess Tyrande Whisperwind, white-haired and silver-eyed, armored in moonlight; and Malfurion's twin brother Illidan — amber-eyed where his people's are silver, gifted, envious, and hungry for power; a love triangle and a moral fault-line in one family. Beside them fought the demigod Cenarius, the Wild Gods, and all five dragonflights.

Then came the betrayal this whole site keeps circling. Neltharion, ten thousand years into the Old Gods' whispers, proposed a weapon to end the war: a golden disc into which each flight poured a portion of its essence. He named it the Dragon Soul. When he unleashed it, he turned it on demon and dragon alike — nearly annihilating the blue flight in a single stroke — declared himself Deathwing the Destroyer, and shattered the unity of dragonkind forever. The disc, later renamed the Demon Soul, holds the power of all five flights except his own; remember it, because it returns twice in later chapters, once to wound him and once to kill him.

Illidan, meanwhile, played his own double game with the Legion, and in the war's aftermath committed a sin his people could not forgive — recreating a new Well of Eternity from stolen vials of the old one's waters. His brother imprisoned him beneath the mountains for ten thousand years. File that away for Chapter VIII.

The Sundering

The defenders' final gambit was to destroy the Well itself and sever the Legion's anchor to the world. It worked — catastrophically. The Well's implosion tore the single continent apart into the seas and scattered landmasses of the modern map: Kalimdor in the west, the Eastern Kingdoms in the east, frozen Northrend at the top of the world. This is the Great Sundering, the calendar-zero of Warcraft history. Azshara and her drowning court were caught at the bottom of the new sea — not by death, but by N'Zoth, who remade them into the naga — sea-serpents with torsos and arms, finned and scaled, their queen grown titanic and many-armed. Azshara traded one dark patron for another, and settled in to scheme for ten thousand years.

Nordrassil and the long vigil

On the new world's tallest peak, Mount Hyjal, the surviving night elves planted a colossal World Tree, Nordrassil, over the scarred remnant of the Well. Alexstrasza, Ysera, and Nozdormu together blessed it — and through Ysera's blessing the night elves were granted immortality so long as the tree stood. The kaldorei turned their backs on arcane magic, embraced Cenarius's druidism, and kept a quiet vigil over the world's wounds. The age of empires gave way to long, slow millennia — which is exactly where the next chapter picks up.

New to Warcraft? If you remember one thing: the Sundering is the "before" and "after" line of this whole setting. Every date you'll ever see in Warcraft lore is measured against either this event or the opening of the Dark Portal in Chapter VI.
