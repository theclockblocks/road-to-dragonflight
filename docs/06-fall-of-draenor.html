VIAnother world entirely

The Fall of Draenor

The orcs, the draenei, a demon's bargain, and the opening of the Dark Portal

Leave Azeroth for a moment. On a savage, beautiful world called Draenor, two peoples are about to be destroyed by the same lie.

Two peoples

The orcs of Draenor lived in shamanistic clans — massive, tusked, brown-skinned hunters and mystics bound to the elements, honorable by their own hard code. (Yes, brown: the green comes later, and it is not natural.) Their neighbors were refugees: the draenei, "exiled ones" — tall, blue-to-lavender skinned, with cranial crests, tendrils, tails, and hooves, their cities grown from glowing crystal — a fragment of the eredar race who had fled their homeworld of Argus twenty-five thousand years earlier, when Sargeras came recruiting. Their leader, the prophet Velen — ancient, white-bearded, his milky eyes seeing the future more clearly than the room — had refused the bargain his two closest friends accepted — Kil'jaeden and Archimonde, who became the Legion's greatest lieutenants. Guided by the Light-beings called naaru, Velen's people had run from world to world for millennia. On Draenor, at last, they thought they were safe.

Kil'jaeden never stopped hunting them. And when the Legion found Draenor, it chose not to invade but to arrange things — because a corrupted people makes a better weapon than a conquered one.

The blood of Mannoroth

Working through the orcs' own spiritual leaders — first the elder shaman Ner'zhul, who balked when he glimpsed the truth, then his ruthless apprentice Gul'dan — a hunched, hooded figure bent over a skull-topped staff, fel-green light leaking from his eyes — who did not — Kil'jaeden fed the clans a story that the draenei were plotting their destruction. Shamanism withered; Gul'dan's new warlock magic spread, and the fel energies seeping through the clans began turning brown skin sickly green even in those who never touched it. Then, at a gathering of the clans, the chieftains drank the blood of the demon lord Mannoroth — a pit lord, a tusked dinosaur bulk fused to a giant's torso — binding themselves body and soul to the Legion's bloodlust. The transformation finished overnight: green skin, furnace-red eyes, and a hunger that had to go somewhere. Of the great chieftains, only Durotan of the Frostwolves refused the cup.

What followed was genocide. The united Horde — a name coined by Gul'dan's puppet warchief — fell upon the draenei and slaughtered them almost to extinction, Velen and a remnant fleeing into hiding. But a weapon, once used, needs a new target, and the demon-blood fury did not fade. The orcs began to turn on each other. Gul'dan needed a new war — and his true master, speaking through a sleeping Guardian on a world called Azeroth, offered him one.

The Dark Portal

Sargeras-in-Medivh reached across the void to Gul'dan and struck the bargain that starts the Warcraft franchise proper: power for the warlock, an army for the titan. On one side stood the mightiest mortal mage of Azeroth; on the other, the warlocks of a corrupted world. Together they tore open a wound between the two planets — the Dark Portal — and the starving, blood-cursed Horde marched through it into the quiet southern forests of Stormwind.

New to Warcraft? This is the setting's second great "before and after" line. Warcraft dates everything from either the Sundering or this moment — the opening of the Dark Portal, year zero of the modern era.
