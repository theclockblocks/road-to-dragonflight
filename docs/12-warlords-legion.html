XIIWarlords of Draenor & Legion

The Legion's Last Crusade

A timeline stolen, a demon army's final invasion, the death of a Dreamer — and a sword through the world

Two expansions that form one story: the Legion's long-promised final invasion — and the wound it leaves in the planet on its way out.

Warlords of Draenor: the stolen timeline

Awaiting trial, Garrosh is broken out by a rogue bronze dragon and escapes somewhere no manhunt can follow: an alternate timeline's Draenor, thirty-five years in its past, before the orcs ever drank demon blood. There he arms his father's uncorrupted generation with modern war-machines and forges them into the Iron Horde — a conquering army meant to redo history with the demon's bargain refused and the genocide kept. Azeroth's heroes invade the alternate world to stop him; Garrosh dies in a duel with Thrall; and the campaign's endgame goes to a familiar face, for in every timeline there is a Gul'dan. This one, spurned by his own people, summons the Legion in full — and when alternate-Draenor is saved anyway, Gul'dan is hurled by Kil'jaeden through the timeways into the main universe, with one job: open the door.

The expansion is mechanically beloved and narratively awkward — a time-travel cul-de-sac — but its consequences are enormous: it hands the main timeline the architect of its final Legion war, and later, in Legion's aftermath, it delivers an entire population of uncorrupted orcs (the Mag'har) into the Horde.

Legion: the third invasion

Gul'dan raises the Tomb of Sargeras — the sunken vault his prime-timeline self died opening in Chapter VII — and through it the Burning Legion invades Azeroth at a scale dwarfing every previous war. The opening battle at the Broken Shore is a massacre of leaders: King Varian and Warchief Vol'jin both die, the crown passing to Varian's gentle son Anduin and — fatefully — the warchief's mantle to Sylvanas. Adventurers rise as leaders of their class orders, wielding legendary artifact weapons, and the war runs on three great rails:

  • The Emerald Nightmare. The satyr lord Xavius corrupts the Emerald Dream itself, and Ysera the Dreamer — poisoned, turned against everything she is — must be put down by Tyrande, the closest thing the setting has to a mercy-killing of a goddess by her own priestess. Her spirit passes into the Dream; her daughter Merithra is left to inherit a grief the size of a flight.
  • Illidan's return. The demon hunters resurrect their master, and Illidan — vindicated as the one who always understood what the Legion required — becomes the war's ruthless prophet. Kil'jaeden is finally slain aboard his own command ship, admitting with his dying breath that he had always known Sargeras's bargain was a lie; and Illidan, characteristically, uses the moment to tear open a permanent portal to Argus, the Legion's throneworld, hanging it in Azeroth's sky. No more repelling invasions: the war goes to them.
  • Argus. On the draenei's lost homeworld — revealed to be a tortured world-soul of its own, its titan-spirit enslaved as the Legion's resurrection engine — the heroes free the spirits of the Pantheon, and Sargeras himself is finally seized and imprisoned by his restored kin at the seat of the Pantheon.

The sword

He gets one last swing. As the Pantheon drags him from reality, Sargeras drives his colossal blade into Azeroth — a continent-sized sword impaling the desert of Silithus, wounding the world-soul herself. The planet bleeds: her golden lifeblood wells up through the wound as a crystalline substance the dwarves name Azerite — raw, portable, weaponizable god-blood. The Legion is finished forever. The peace lasts exactly as long as it takes both factions to understand what Azerite is worth.
