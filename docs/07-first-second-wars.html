VIIYear 0 – 8 after the Portal

The First and Second Wars

Stormwind falls, the Alliance rises, Draenor dies — and a captive soul is forged into the Lich King

These are the events of the original two strategy games, compressed to what still matters. By this chapter's end, every doom of the next twenty years will already be in motion.

The First War — the fall of Stormwind

The Horde came up out of the southern swamps, and the kingdom of Stormwind went to meet it, and lost. That much is an ordinary war story. What is not ordinary is why.

On paper Stormwind should have held. It was a serious kingdom with a serious army, and the orcs — savage, certainly, but riven by clan feuds and fighting on ground they had never seen — were not the better force. The humans kept losing anyway, and for years nobody could work out how. Intelligence went astray. Defenses failed in ways that made no sense. Every plan the crown made seemed to reach the enemy before it reached the front. The answer was that the kingdom's single greatest asset was also its leak: Medivh, the last Guardian of Tirisfal, the most powerful mortal alive — and, since before his birth, the chair Sargeras had been sitting in.

Two men worked it out. Anduin Lothar, the finest soldier of his generation and Medivh's oldest friend, and Khadgar, a young apprentice the Guardian had taken in — who had been quietly noticing things he was not meant to notice. They climbed the tower at Karazhan and killed him. It was not a clean fight or a triumphant one: at the very end Medivh surfaced — the real one, the friend, appalled at what had been done with his hands — and the dying magic aged Khadgar into an old man's body where he stood. He was in his twenties. He never got the years back. Sargeras's spirit was driven out into the shadows, which is not the same as destroyed. Merely inconvenienced.

It came too late for the kingdom. A half-orc interpreter named Garona — planted in the court, and compelled by a spell she could not refuse — reached King Llane in his own throne room, and the capital fell within the year. Lothar put what was left of his people on ships: soldiers, refugees, and the murdered king's seven-year-old son, a boy named Varian, watching his father's city burn from the deck. They sailed north across the sea to Lordaeron, where Lothar arrived at another king's court as a beggar with a warning — a hard thing for a proud man to be.

The Second War — the Alliance of Lordaeron

Confronted with the refugees' testimony, King Terenas Menethil II of Lordaeron managed what none of his ancestors ever had: he got the human kingdoms to agree. The Seven Kingdoms, the dwarves and gnomes, and — reluctantly, honoring a debt four thousand years old from the troll wars of Chapter V — the high elves of Quel'Thalas came under one banner, the Alliance of Lordaeron. The elves did the minimum, and left early, and left angry. File that away: it is the opening move of a very slow divorce.

Against them stood a Horde at its terrible height under Warchief Orgrim Doomhammer — worth understanding, because he is not a monster. He was a competent commander of a corrupted army: a Blackrock orc who had spent years watching warlocks run his people through a puppet warchief, and who finally killed the puppet, took the ancestral hammer, and tried to fight a real war with what the warlocks had left him. Twice it looked as though he would win it.

It turned on treachery. At the decisive hour, with the outcome genuinely in doubt, Gul'dan took a third of the army off the field to chase a sunken tomb he believed held a god's power. It held his death instead — his own demons tore him apart in the dark — and the missing third cost the Horde the war. Lothar died at the foot of Blackrock mountain, still fighting, and never saw the alliance he had built hold together; his lieutenant Turalyon picked up the broken banner and finished the campaign, breaking the Horde at the gates of its last redoubt. The Dark Portal was thrown down.

The surviving orcs were penned in internment camps, and there something happened that nobody had planned for. Cut off from demonic power for the first time in a generation, they went grey. They sat down. They stopped wanting anything at all. It was withdrawal — but their jailers saw docility, wrote the orc problem down as solved, and stopped watching the camps. Among the prisoners was a boy raised from infancy by a human lord as a slave and a gladiator, and given a slave's name for what he was: Thrall, son of the murdered Frostwolf chieftain Durotan. Remember him; he's the future.

The death of Draenor

Back on the orcs' dying homeworld, Ner'zhul — the old shaman Gul'dan had betrayed and supplanted — made one final grab for greatness, tearing open portals to new worlds for the remnant Horde to flee through. The overlapping portal energies ripped Draenor apart. An Alliance expedition (Khadgar and Turalyon among them) was stranded on the far side as the world shattered into the floating wreckage later called Outland.

The forging of the Lich King

Ner'zhul escaped the explosion — into Kil'jaeden's waiting hands. There is a grim symmetry worth naming here: every version of this man is a victim of the one before it. Deceived by Kil'jaeden, supplanted by his own apprentice, and now collected by the demon he first said no to. None of which stops any of it from being his fault. The Legion had one more use for the old shaman. His body was destroyed and his spirit bound into a suit of armor and a runeblade, sealed inside a block of ice, and hurled down onto the frozen roof of Azeroth, Northrend. His purpose: to spread a plague of undeath and raise an army — the Scourge — to soften Azeroth for the Legion's next invasion. His new name was the Lich King, and his prison-throne of ice held two artifacts you must remember: the Helm of Domination that housed his will, and a hungering sword, waiting to be found, named Frostmourne.
