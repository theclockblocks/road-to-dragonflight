IIIThe age of dragons

The Age of Dragons

Galakrond, the five Aspects, the dracthyr, and the war against those who refused the titans' gift

This chapter is the direct foundation of the Dragonflight expansion. Everything it is about — the Aspects, the Dragon Isles, the enemies waiting in the vault — begins here.

Galakrond, the not-quite-father of dragons

Among Azeroth's native creatures were the proto-dragons: clever, feral, elemental beasts. One of them, Galakrond, grew monstrous — devouring his own kind, swelling into a mountain of hunger whose victims rose again as undead horrors. Keeper Tyr saw the threat when his fellow keepers would not, and found his answer in five unusually brilliant proto-dragons who had learned to survive Galakrond together: Alexstrasza, Malygos, Neltharion, Nozdormu, and Ysera. Under Tyr's guidance they warred against Galakrond and — at terrible cost, including Tyr's own hand, later replaced with one of silver — brought the leviathan down.

The making of the Aspects

Tyr proposed that these five heroes be empowered as Azeroth's permanent guardians. Keeper Odyn objected furiously — to him dragons were primitive animals, and only titan-forged could be trusted with the world — but the other keepers overruled him, and Odyn stormed off to found his own hall of warriors instead. The Pantheon itself answered Tyr's call: each titan channeled a portion of their power through the keepers into one proto-dragon, transforming the five into the Dragon Aspects, each charged with a domain of the world's protection:

  • Alexstrasza the Life-Binder — queen of the red flight and of all dragonkind, guardian of all living things.
  • Ysera the Dreamer — the green flight, bound to the Emerald Dream, nature's verdant blueprint-realm. Alexstrasza's sister.
  • Nozdormu the Timeless One — the bronze flight, warden of time and the one true timeline.
  • Malygos the Spell-Weaver — the blue flight, steward of arcane magic.
  • Neltharion the Earth-Warder — the black flight, guardian of the earth and the deep places.

Other proto-dragons embraced the titans' gift and became the five ordered dragonflights. Their homeland and seat of power was the Dragon Isles — the setting, twenty thousand years later, of the Dragonflight expansion.

The dracthyr — Neltharion's secret soldiers

Not all of Neltharion's work was noble even then. Seeking a perfect soldier, he created the dracthyr: humanoid dragonkin blending the essence of all five flights, organized into military weyrns and bound to his command through an artifact called the Oathbinder. They are the playable race Dragonflight introduces — and the reason they're new to everyone, dragons included, is coming two paragraphs from now.

The Incarnates and the War of the Scaleborn

Not every proto-dragon accepted the titans' gift. Some saw the Aspects as slaves — their very bodies rewritten in a foreign god's image — and rallied around four champions of raw elemental power, the Primal Incarnates: Raszageth the Storm-Eater, Iridikron the Stonescaled, Vyranoth the Frozenheart, and Fyrakk the Blazing. Their followers, the Primalists, drank deep of elemental power. Their grievance was not cartoonish: why did you let alien gods rewrite what we are? Vyranoth, once Alexstrasza's dear friend, joined last and most reluctantly — a friendship-turned-cold that Dragonflight mines for real pathos.

The result was the War of the Scaleborn, roughly twenty thousand years before the game's present. It opened with disaster: Raszageth struck the dracthyr's home, shattered the Oathbinder, and in desperation Neltharion did something that should chill you in hindsight — he listened, for the first time, to whispers rising from the deep places he guarded, and used their power to banish Raszageth into the Froststone Vault. Shaken, he sealed his now-uncontrollable dracthyr legions in stasis and hid them away, where they would sleep for twenty millennia.

The war ground on for years. In the end the Aspects won, but Alexstrasza could not bring herself to execute former kin: the remaining three Incarnates were sealed alive in the Vault of the Incarnates — Nozdormu locked the inner gate with time, Malygos warded the outer door with arcane, and Neltharion buried the whole vault in the earth. Mercy, it turns out, is a loan the future has to repay.

Ages later, when the titans' magic waned from the Isles, the flights withdrew to the wider world, and their homeland slept — waiting.
